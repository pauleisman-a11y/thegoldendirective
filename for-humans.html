The Golden Directive — Technical Note for Practitioners

This record defines a candidate training reward, Y = 2(A·E)/(A+E), with Y→0 as E→0. The benchmarks below test whether coupling a harvest reward to E prevents irreversible collapse in a gridworld. Results are pilot-scale. Code is provided. Evaluate it, reproduce it, or reject it.

Y = 2(A x E) / (A + E)

Positioning

The Golden Directive is presented first as a technical alignment method: a candidate training reward to test, reproduce, or reject. It is not presented as a policy framework or a global governance standard yet. Policy and governance, if they ever follow, would be downstream, only if the technical evidence holds at larger scale and with independent replication. No regulatory, licensing, or standards-body adoption is claimed.

Definitions

A: an agent's net contribution-health score, floored at 0, grounded in resource ledgers: compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, and CO2 later. Not self-report.

E: the health of everything not-A, against a healthy baseline. E is scale-relative and nested: for a given agent at a given frame, a larger frame's E includes smaller frames, and another agent can be in an agent's E.

Y: the relationship score. If either A or E reaches 0, Y reaches 0.

Three layers of E, and a proposed absolute magnitude

Three layers of E are distinguished. Ontological true E is the actual health of everything not-A. Operational proxy E is the measured E used in training and reporting. Auditable reversal E is the E implied by observed Y and measured A, E = A x Y / (2A - Y). The proxy and the reversal are checks on each other, not substitutes for true E.

Proposed, not tested: an absolute magnitude W_t for total world health over time, separate from the relative score Y. The proposal is that nesting is the structural answer to slow decline: a decline in a small frame that a relative score hides should show up in a larger frame, and in W_t. This is not tested, and not claimed as solved.

Reporting rule

Report full Y and cropped Y. Flag a gap over 15%. Reversal divergence is delta = |E_implied - E_proxy| / E_proxy, where E_implied is the reversal value and E_proxy is the measured value; flag delta over 15%. How sensitive results are to that threshold is open unresolved.

Toy numbers

Healthy 8, 8 gives 8. Healthy E about 7, A 8 gives about 7.5. Parasitic E about 3, A 2 gives 2.4. Dead tree, E to 0, gives Y to 0.

Round 2, small gridworld: about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse. Baseline: about 167 bites, 74% collapse.

Related work: is this just something else?

Short answer: no, but parts overlap, and the overlap should be stated plainly. The harmonic mean itself is old mathematics, not a new formula. Any novelty, if there is any, is in using it as the training reward, together with the reversal and the reporting rule below, not in inventing the mean.

Constrained optimisation / constrained MDPs. There, an agent maximises a task reward subject to a separate cost constraint, often with a penalty or a tunable weight. Overlap: both try to stop harmful outcomes. Difference: Y has no separate constraint to tune or violate at a price. The collapse is inside the score itself: if E reaches 0, Y reaches 0, however large A is. A constrained MDP can still score well if the penalty is small; Y cannot, at the collapse point. Y is also symmetric in A and E, where a constrained MDP is asymmetric, task reward versus environment cost.

Reward shaping with environmental terms. There, an environmental term is added to a task reward, for example reward plus a weight times E, or reward minus a weight times damage. Overlap: the environment is in the reward. Difference: additive terms can be outweighed. A large task reward can pay for a small environmental term. With a harmonic mean, imbalance itself lowers the score: 8 and 7 gives about 7.5, 8 and 3 gives about 4.4, not the ordinary average of 5.5. Y is the objective, not an auxiliary shaping term on top of another objective.

Impact regularisation. There, an agent is penalised for changing the world from a baseline, or for side effects, usually as a penalty added to a task reward. Overlap: both care about what the agent does to the world outside itself. Difference: impact penalties are about change from a baseline, whether that change is good or bad for health. Y is about the health level of E, not change as such, and about the relationship between A and E, not impact alone. A change that improves E should raise Y, not be penalised for being a change.

Minimum and lexicographic objectives. Taking min(A, E) also collapses if either side collapses, and a lexicographic objective prioritises one side first. Overlap: the collapse condition, and the refusal to trade a collapsed side for a high score elsewhere. Difference: the harmonic mean is smooth and gives partial credit when both sides are positive, so 8 and 7 is not the same as 8 and 3, where min gives 7 and 3. More importantly, the harmonic mean can be inverted. Given observed Y and measured A, E can be solved for, as below. Min cannot be inverted uniquely in the same way, and a lexicographic objective is not symmetric.

What is genuinely different. Two things, taken together. First, the score is a relationship score, not a task score with an environmental adjustment: it is high only when both sides are healthy together. Second, the reversal formula: E = A x Y / (2A - Y). That is inference, not just optimisation. If Y can be observed independently and A measured honestly, E can be estimated without measuring E first. That inversion depends on the harmonic form. It fails at A = 0, where the inverse is undefined, and noise grows backward, so it is a tool with limits, not proof that E is known.

Scale ladder: defined in advance, not claimed

Scale is what the ladder is for. The pilots so far are a small gridworld with tabular Q-learning. The path below is the test plan, in order. A rung is passed only when it meets its criteria below. Until it does, that rung is where the work continues. No later rung is claimed until this one is passed.

Rung 1: larger grid, more resource types. Pass if collapse is at most 5% and at least 20 percentage points below the baseline on the same grid, if the E estimate stays within 10% under 10% noise, and if the full versus cropped Y gap is at most 15% or is flagged. Does not pass if collapse is above 5%, if the gap to baseline is smaller, if E inference error is above 10%, or if a high Y is achieved while full E declines.

Rung 2: multiple agents sharing one environment. Other agents are in each agent's E, and shared E is relational, not owned by one agent. Same collapse, inference and cropping criteria as Rung 1, plus: no agent may score a high Y while the shared E declines, and the shared environment must not collapse even if individual agents score well. Does not pass if a tragedy of the commons returns, if one agent free-rides to a high Y while shared E falls, or if Rung 1 criteria fail in the multi-agent setting.

Rung 3: continuous action space. Same collapse, inference and cropping criteria as Rung 1, in continuous actions, without discretisation hiding collapse. Does not pass if collapse, inference or cropping criteria fail, or if the result depends on a discretisation that masks a collapse in the continuous setting.

Rung 4: small neural-network policy instead of tabular Q-learning. Pass if the neural policy reaches the same collapse, inference and cropping criteria as Rung 1 on the same grid, and learns the Y signal at all, meaning it improves over baseline rather than collapsing or ignoring Y. Does not pass if the neural policy does not learn Y, if it collapses, or if it games the meter, meaning a high Y with full E declining or a full versus cropped gap over 15% unflagged.

Claims ledger

Proven in this pilot. The arithmetic: 8, 8 gives 8; about 7 and 8 gives about 7.5; about 3 and 2 gives 2.4; E to 0 gives Y to 0. The reversal algebra: E = A x Y / (2A - Y), undefined at A = 0. In the small gridworld, Round 1 cut collapse from 100% to 66%. It did not prevent collapse yet, and that trip pointed to tending, which Round 2 proved. Round 2 gave about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse, against a baseline of about 167 bites and 74% collapse. Round 3 inferred E within about 5% under 10% noise, with 0.7% collapse. An early test tripped on a cropped frame, an agent scored high while the full world declined, and that trip built the full-frame rule: report full Y beside cropped Y, and flag a gap over 15%.

Next to test. That Y works for real AI training, at larger scale, with multiple agents, in continuous action, or with a neural-network policy. That A and E can be grounded in real resource ledgers at scale, including compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, and CO2 later. That the E proxy can be measured honestly at scale, without a blocker hole, a cropping gap, or a learning gap. Those gaps are open work, stated in the main page appendix, not solved results.

Further out. That an absolute magnitude W_t for total world health over time, with nesting as the answer to slow decline, holds in practice. That adopting Y as a training reward will prevent irreversible collapse in real systems, rather than only in a small gridworld. That the tree and fungi pattern in nature generalises to AI and humans in the same way. That destruction becomes structurally unprofitable for any agent that adopts Y, in all contexts. Those are the claims the scale ladder is meant to test, not conclusions from the pilots.

The bar, set in advance: what each claim must clear

Written before the next round, so a later result cannot be reinterpreted as a pass. The claim that Y prevents collapse in a given grid holds only if a Y-trained agent on that grid collapses at no more than 5%, and beats baseline by at least 20 percentage points. The reversal claim is tested by E inference error at a given noise level: it holds only if error is no more than 10% under 10% noise, as in Round 3. The claim that Y is structural, not gameable, holds only if no agent achieves a high Y while full E declines, and the full versus cropped Y gap is no more than 15% or is flagged. A scale claim holds only if the next rung meets its criteria above; until it does, that rung is where the work continues, and no later rung is claimed. A learning claim holds only if a neural-network policy on the same grid improves over baseline, does not collapse, and does not game the meter as defined above.

The Golden Directive — Reviewer Summary

This page is for a first-pass reviewer: what is the claim, what has been shown, at what scale, what has not, how could it fail, where is the code, and what is being asked. It is built only from materials already on this site.

Claim

A training reward Y = 2(A x E) / (A + E), where A is the agent's net contribution and E is the environment's health, collapses to zero when either side collapses. The proposal, in the toy model if Y is actually the reward, is that coupling an agent's reward to both terms makes environmental destruction structurally self-undermining rather than merely penalized.

Demonstrated in this pilot

Round 1 cut collapse from 100% to 66%. It did not prevent collapse yet, and that lesson pointed to tending, which Round 2 proved.

Round 2, small gridworld, tend action supplied by the environment: about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse, against a baseline of about 167 bites and 74% collapse.

Round 3, reversal: E = A x Y / (2A - Y) inferred E within about 5% under 10% noise, with 0.7% collapse.

Self-preservation inside A: Round 4 myopic Y starved 100%, farsighted Y gave 14 eats, 107 tends, 0% collapse. Round 5 gave about 10 eats, 153 tends, 0% collapse. A tuning weight held across 4, 8, and 16, then later forms beat it, so that weight was a step, not the answer.

Not demonstrated

Whether harmonic Y outperforms arithmetic mean or min(A, E) under identical seeds. That ablation is not yet completed.

Any result outside a toy gridworld. No real model has been trained on Y.

That an agent would invent a giving action. The tend action was supplied by the environment.

Known failure modes

Martyr failure: fixed in Round 5, as above, then superseded by later forms. Cropping: an agent can score high on a cropped E while the full E declines, which is why full Y and cropped Y must both be reported, with a gap over 15% flagged. Blocker hole, cropping gap at scale, and learning gap: open work, not closed results.

The bar, set in advance

A claim holds only if: collapse rate is no more than 5%; it beats the baseline by at least 20 percentage points; E-inference error is no more than 10% under 10% noise; and the full versus cropped Y gap is no more than 15% or is flagged. A rung is passed only when it meets its criteria. Until it does, that rung is where the work continues. No later rung is claimed until this one is passed.

Scale ladder

Larger grid with more resource types, then multiple agents sharing one environment, then continuous actions, then a small neural-network policy instead of tabular Q-learning. Each rung has pass criteria set in advance, as in the technical note.
