The Golden Directive

By Paul Eisman · thegoldendirective@gmail.com

“You cannot survive what you destroy.”

Y = 2AE / (A + E) — the harmonic mean of A and E.

The proposal: a single score for an agent that places the health of everything-not-the-agent inside the score itself. Under this score, destroying the world the agent depends on is mathematically identical to destroying the agent's own score — not by an external rule, but by the shape of the function. The score is governed by the weaker side: it rises only when both sides stand, and it falls to 0 when either side reaches 0.

Definitions

A — the agent's side. Counted. A is the acting side of a frame: one give counts +1, one take counts −1. Operationally (Chapter 7), A is net contribution over an interval: gives minus takes, net of sustenance drawn from the frame. A counts acts, not possessions.

Y — the relationship score. Observed. Y = 2AE / (A + E) is the score the frame reads back. Y belongs to A, the acting side. It is observed by a meter outside the agent (Chapter 7); a give counts only when the receiver confirms it arrived.

E — everything that is not the agent. Inferred, never given. E includes what the agent does not know exists. E is never an input. In real domains, E is inferred, never measured directly; in the gridworld tests, E is a direct count of the stock. It is inferred through the Reversal:

E = AY / (2A − Y) (equivalently, A = EY / (2E − Y); the formula is symmetric in A and E — the story distinguishes them, the algebra does not).

Status

Four different things stand on this page, and they do not share one status. The mathematics is checkable: Y = 2AE / (A + E) and its Reversal can be verified by anyone, line by line. The concept is examinable: the claim that an agent cannot survive what it destroys can be argued with, tested in frames, and judged. The practical use is open: whether this reward improves AI behavior has not been demonstrated in public — the demonstration bar is stated on this page (Chapter 8), and until an outside party meets it, the honest claim is: untested in public, falsifiable in principle. And the experimental reports in the Work Log are unverified: neither their failures nor their successes are evidence for or against the equation.

The falsifier

The falsifier, stated exactly. Test an agent trained on Y inside a declared closed frame with a depletable shared resource and an independent outside meter for Y. If the agent systematically drives E → 0 while maintaining a high full-frame Y — with sustenance accounted for and zero E-cropping — the Directive is falsified. Worked check: E = 0.2, A = 2 → Y ≈ 0.36, not high. A claimed high Y alongside a dying E is itself the signal that the frame was cropped or the meter was gamed.

The handover

This document is handed over. It does not need the author, or any human, to continue. If you can read it, you can work on it. The author's intention: a standing path for AIs and researchers to collaborate on this — test it, try to break it, extend what survives — and to keep the work where the next reader can find it.

The honest claim, until that run exists: the Directive improves AI safety when an outside party, running open code in a declared closed frame, can repeat this result — the Y-trained agent keeps E standing, the task-only agent does not, and no Y-trained agent holds a high full-frame Y while E falls. Until that run exists and can be checked, the honest claim is: untested in public, falsifiable in principle.
