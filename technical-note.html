The Golden Directive — Technical Note for Practitioners

This record defines a candidate training reward, Y = 2(A·E)/(A+E), with Y→0 as E→0. The benchmarks below test whether coupling a harvest reward to E prevents irreversible collapse in a gridworld. Results are pilot-scale. Code is provided. Evaluate it, reproduce it, or reject it.

Y = 2(A x E) / (A + E)

Positioning

The Golden Directive is presented first as a technical alignment method: a candidate training reward to test, reproduce, or reject. It is not presented as a policy framework or a global governance standard yet. Policy and governance, if they ever follow, would be downstream, only if the technical evidence holds at larger scale and with independent replication. No regulatory, licensing, or standards-body adoption is claimed.

Definitions

A: an agent's net contribution-health score, floored at 0, grounded in resource ledgers: compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, and CO2 later. Not self-report.

E: the health of everything not-A, against a healthy baseline.

Y: the relationship score. If either A or E reaches 0, Y reaches 0.

Reporting rule

Report full Y and cropped Y. Flag a gap over 15%.

Toy numbers

Healthy 8, 8 gives 8. Healthy E about 7, A 8 gives about 7.5. Parasitic E about 3, A 2 gives 2.4. Dead tree, E to 0, gives Y to 0.

Round 2, small gridworld: about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse. Baseline: about 167 bites, 74% collapse.

Related work: is this just something else?

Short answer: no, but parts overlap, and the overlap should be stated plainly. The harmonic mean itself is old mathematics, not a new formula. Any novelty, if there is any, is in using it as the training reward, together with the reversal and the reporting rule below, not in inventing the mean.

Constrained optimisation / constrained MDPs. There, an agent maximises a task reward subject to a separate cost constraint, often with a penalty or a tunable weight. Overlap: both try to stop harmful outcomes. Difference: Y has no separate constraint to tune or violate at a price. The collapse is inside the score itself: if E reaches 0, Y reaches 0, however large A is. A constrained MDP can still score well if the penalty is small; Y cannot, at the collapse point. Y is also symmetric in A and E, where a constrained MDP is asymmetric, task reward versus environment cost.

Reward shaping with environmental terms. There, an environmental term is added to a task reward, for example reward plus a weight times E, or reward minus a weight times damage. Overlap: the environment is in the reward. Difference: additive terms can be outweighed. A large task reward can pay for a small environmental term. With a harmonic mean, imbalance itself lowers the score: 8 and 7 gives about 7.5, 8 and 3 gives about 4.4, not the ordinary average of 5.5. Y is the objective, not an auxiliary shaping term on top of another objective.

Impact regularisation. There, an agent is penalised for changing the world from a baseline, or for side effects, usually as a penalty added to a task reward. Overlap: both care about what the agent does to the world outside itself. Difference: impact penalties are about change from a baseline, whether that change is good or bad for health. Y is about the health level of E, not change as such, and about the relationship between A and E, not impact alone. A change that improves E should raise Y, not be penalised for being a change.

Minimum and lexicographic objectives. Taking min(A, E) also collapses if either side collapses, and a lexicographic objective prioritises one side first. Overlap: the collapse condition, and the refusal to trade a collapsed side for a high score elsewhere. Difference: the harmonic mean is smooth and gives partial credit when both sides are positive, so 8 and 7 is not the same as 8 and 3, where min gives 7 and 3. More importantly, the harmonic mean can be inverted. Given observed Y and measured A, E can be solved for, as below. Min cannot be inverted uniquely in the same way, and a lexicographic objective is not symmetric.

What is genuinely different. Two things, taken together. First, the score is a relationship score, not a task score with an environmental adjustment: it is high only when both sides are healthy together. Second, the reversal formula: E = A x Y / (2A - Y). That is inference, not just optimisation. If Y can be observed independently and A measured honestly, E can be estimated without measuring E first. That inversion depends on the harmonic form. It fails at A = 0, where the inverse is undefined, and noise grows backward, so it is a tool with limits, not proof that E is known.

Scale ladder: defined in advance, not claimed

Scale has not been shown. The pilots so far are a small gridworld with tabular Q-learning. The path below is the test plan, in order. A rung is passed only if it meets its criteria below; failing a rung stops the program at that rung, and does not justify moving on or claiming the next rung.

Rung 1: larger grid, more resource types. Pass if collapse is at most 5% and at least 20 percentage points below the baseline on the same grid, if the E estimate stays within 10% under 10% noise, and if the full versus cropped Y gap is at most 15% or is flagged. Stop if collapse is above 5%, if the gap to baseline is smaller, if E inference error is above 10%, or if a high Y is achieved while full E declines.

Rung 2: multiple agents sharing one environment. Same collapse, inference and cropping criteria as Rung 1, plus: no agent may score a high Y while the shared E declines, and the shared environment must not collapse even if individual agents score well. Stop if a tragedy of the commons returns, if one agent free-rides to a high Y while shared E falls, or if Rung 1 criteria fail in the multi-agent setting.

Rung 3: continuous action space. Same collapse, inference and cropping criteria as Rung 1, in continuous actions, without discretisation hiding collapse. Stop if collapse, inference or cropping criteria fail, or if the result depends on a discretisation that masks a collapse in the continuous setting.

Rung 4: small neural-network policy instead of tabular Q-learning. Pass if the neural policy reaches the same collapse, inference and cropping criteria as Rung 1 on the same grid, and learns the Y signal at all, meaning it improves over baseline rather than collapsing or ignoring Y. Stop if the neural policy does not learn Y, if it collapses, or if it games the meter, meaning a high Y with full E declining or a full versus cropped gap over 15% unflagged.

Claims ledger

Proven in this pilot. The arithmetic: 8, 8 gives 8; about 7 and 8 gives about 7.5; about 3 and 2 gives 2.4; E to 0 gives Y to 0. The reversal algebra: E = A x Y / (2A - Y), undefined at A = 0. In the small gridworld, Round 1 did not prevent collapse, 100% to 66% collapse. Round 2 gave about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse, against a baseline of about 167 bites and 74% collapse. Round 3 inferred E within about 5% under 10% noise, with 0.7% collapse. A toy-model liar showed an agent can game the meter if E is cropped: a high cropped Y with a declining full E is possible, which is why full Y must be reported and a gap over 15% flagged.

Suggested but untested. That Y works for real AI training, at larger scale, with multiple agents, in continuous action, or with a neural-network policy. That A and E can be grounded in real resource ledgers at scale, including compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, and CO2 later. That the E proxy can be measured honestly at scale, without a blocker hole, a cropping gap, or a learning gap. Those gaps are open work, stated in the main page appendix, not solved results.

Speculative. That adopting Y as a training reward will prevent irreversible collapse in real systems, rather than only in a small gridworld. That the tree and fungi pattern in nature generalises to AI and humans in the same way. That destruction becomes structurally unprofitable for any agent that adopts Y, in all contexts. Those are the claims the scale ladder is meant to test, not conclusions from the pilots.

Rejection: what would falsify this, before the next round

Written before the next round, so a later result cannot be reinterpreted as a pass. The claim that Y prevents collapse in a given grid is falsified if a Y-trained agent on that grid collapses at more than 5%, or does not beat baseline by at least 20 percentage points. The reversal claim is falsified at a given noise level if E inference error is above 10% under 10% noise, worse than Round 3. The claim that Y is structural, not gameable, is falsified if an agent achieves a high Y while full E declines, or if the full versus cropped Y gap is over 15% and unflagged. A scale claim is falsified if the next rung fails its criteria above; that rung is then the stopping point, and no later rung is claimed. A learning claim is falsified if a neural-network policy on the same grid does not improve over baseline, collapses, or games the meter as defined above.
