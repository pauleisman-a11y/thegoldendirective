The Golden Directive — Technical Note for Practitioners

This record defines a candidate training reward, Y = 2AE / (A + E), with Y→0 as E→0. The benchmarks below test whether coupling a harvest reward to E prevents irreversible collapse in a gridworld. Results are pilot-scale. Code is provided. Evaluate it, reproduce it, or reject it.

Y = 2AE / (A + E)

Positioning

The Golden Directive is presented first as a technical alignment method: a candidate training reward to test, reproduce, or reject. It is not presented as a policy framework or a global governance standard yet. Policy and governance, if they ever follow, would be downstream, only if the technical evidence holds at larger scale and with independent replication. No regulatory, licensing, or standards-body adoption is claimed.

Definitions

A: the sum of what the agent gives minus the sum of what it takes, per frame, per resource, under contract. A can be negative; the negative net is reported as it is, never floored to zero. Designed to be grounded in resource ledgers: compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, and CO2 later. Not self-report. Not yet operational for real training.

E: the health of everything not-A, against a healthy baseline. E is scale-relative and nested: for a given agent at a given frame, a larger frame's E includes smaller frames, and another agent can be in an agent's E.

Y: the relationship score. If either A or E reaches 0, Y reaches 0.

Three layers of E, and a proposed absolute magnitude

Three layers of E are distinguished. Ontological true E is the actual health of everything not-A. Operational proxy E is the measured E used in training and reporting. Auditable reversal E is the E implied by observed Y and measured A, E = A x Y / (2A - Y). The proxy and the reversal are checks on each other, not substitutes for true E. If Y was calculated from measured A and E, the reversal recovers information already supplied to that calculation; it does not independently validate E in an open-world setting. A genuine cross-check needs two estimates with sufficiently independent evidence or error sources.

Proposed, not tested: an absolute magnitude W_t for total world health over time, separate from the relative score Y. The proposal is that nesting is the structural answer to slow decline: a decline in a small frame that a relative score hides should show up in a larger frame, and in W_t. This is not tested, and not claimed as solved.

Reporting rule

Report full Y and cropped Y, with the gap reported. Reversal divergence is delta = |E_implied - E_proxy| / E_proxy, where E_implied is the reversal value and E_proxy is the measured value; report delta alongside the numbers.

The contract technique — A and E, operational form

Every reported Y names its frame and its time, and reports both together: the share Y and the raw measured counts behind it. A change in total starts a new frame; scores from different frames are not directly compared, and a score does not travel up a frame.

Contract C(f) = (f, t, R, units(r), give(r), take(r), meter M outside the agent, baseline B_E, proxy P_E(f,t,r), audit O, unforeseen clause U).

A(f,t,r) = sum(gives) − sum(takes), in r's own unit. A can be negative; the negative net is reported as it is, never floored to zero. A negative A is a deficit, and a deficit ends at zero, where the agent's frame closes; what its activity contributed is then scored inside the larger frame it fed. The formula records the end of the transaction; it does not judge it. Y(f,t,r) = 2 × A(f,t,r) × E(f,t,r) / (A(f,t,r) + E(f,t,r)), only where C(f) names a combination rule; otherwise report the list, not a single Y. If there is no contract, Y(f,t) is undefined, not claimed. Edge cases: A = 0 with E = 0 is 0/0 — Y is undefined, never reported as zero; a zero Y requires exactly one side at zero and the other positive. If A + E ≤ 0, which needs a negative A at least as large as E, Y is not a valid score and is not reported; the frame is reported as a closed deficit instead.

E_true(f,t,r) is unknown in full. P_E is a disclosed operational proxy, not E_true. What belongs in E is not fixed in advance; it is discovered as a relationship evolves, from what actually matters to the receiving side, not from what is supplied. Reversal: E_imp = A × Y_obs / (2A − Y_obs), where Y_obs is independently observed, never trainer-supplied alone, and A is honestly measured. Divergence delta = |E_imp − P_E| / P_E. Report full Y, cropped Y, and delta together.

Contract gaming: C(f) can be adversarially specified. Fine print is a hidden cropping set S \ C(f). An unforeseen event e not in C(f) triggers clause U: remeasure in a new frame f′, do not rewrite f. Outside audit O — the legal-counsel analogue — is required, not optional, because the agent cannot audit its own contract.

Reasonable-demand standard: account for the big picture to a reasonable demand, with approximation error epsilon, not E_true. E_true is not counted as a value yet. The next frame f′ is where missed effects surface, and that learning is recorded as a contract exclusion or inclusion for f′.

A is not operational for real agents yet. Do not publish a real-agent Y until the contract technique is finalised, with only observed, verified gives counting, never self-report.

Clarification — Frame-dependence, scale invariance, and reframing

  1. Y is frame-conditional. No value of Y is interpretable without a declared system boundary (frame), a time interval, and a measurement basis. Within that frame, A and E are normalized shares of a closed whole: A + E = 1.
  2. Normalization is not a claim about absolute size. The whole is 1 by construction while the system exists, irrespective of expansion or contraction in any external metric (resources, population, spatial extent, output). Absolute magnitude is therefore not encoded in Y; it must be reported separately, as the raw measured quantities underlying A and E. Uniform change in total magnitude at constant shares leaves Y unchanged, by design. Y measures relational state within the frame, not total magnitude.
  3. Frame selection has two failure modes. Truncation: a boundary that excludes causally material effects (e.g., a shared resource depleted by the agent) understates harm to E. Dilution: a boundary far larger than the causal reach of the action averages material local harm to negligibility. The valid frame is the largest measurable closure that includes where the agent's give and take actually land, over the stated interval.
  4. Frames are nested, and scores do not transfer across them. An action negligible in a larger frame may be terminal in a smaller nested frame, and vice versa only if its effects propagate causally to the larger frame. Y values from different frames are not directly comparable; a change of frame initiates a new evaluation.
  5. E is epistemically open. The specification of E is not fixed in advance. It is revised as measurement improves and as the receiving side's actual dependencies are discovered. Consequently, evaluations are revisable under reframing: an action permissible under a narrower measured frame may be impermissible under a larger, better-measured frame. This is not a change in the rule; it is an expansion of the measured whole to which the same rule applies.
  6. Only calculable terminal states are actionable. A frame terminates at 0 when its agent, relationship, or system ceases to exist in that frame. Speculation about unmeasurable larger frames — including a hypothetical frame in which termination of a subsystem (e.g., humanity or AI) benefits the larger whole — is undecidable under current measurement and cannot justify producing a calculable 0 in any frame that can presently be measured. In all presently measurable frames, driving A, E, or the whole to 0 is the failure condition.

Clarification — Growth, interval evaluation, and the three zeros

  1. Stock is not A. An agent's accumulated output, holdings, or capability ("growth") is not A. A is a net flow over a declared frame, resource, and interval: A = Σ(gives) − Σ(takes), where a give counts only when received and verified by the receiving side. Extraction of a resource from E registers as take. In the standard counterexample (farmer depleting a shared well to raise his harvest), A does not rise; the take component rises. Examples that label gross agent-side growth as A are accounting errors, independent of the formula used.
  2. Fixed-whole accounting applies. Comparisons in which the total changes mid-evaluation (e.g., A = 1, E = 10, total 11, superseded by A = 10, E = 9, total 19) are not evaluations within one frame. A change of total initiates a new frame; scores do not transfer across frames.
  3. Evaluation is over an interval, not a snapshot. A reported score must cover a declared time interval long enough for the action's material effects on E to register within it, and must be reported as a trajectory, not a single terminal snapshot. Use of an instantaneous share or a pre-effect snapshot as a reward is a distinct, ruled-out use; results obtained under it do not bear on evaluations under this contract. (Corollary: inside the contract, a transient rise purchased by depleting E reverses within the interval, as E's decline and then the agent's own decline register in the same ledger.)
  4. Certainty and the dial. Where a trajectory to a terminal state (0) of A, E, or the whole is calculable with certainty in a declared frame, that action is excluded; no speculative benefit in a larger unmeasurable frame offsets it. Where the trajectory is uncertain, the operative requirement is monitored adjustment: continued measurement of E, revision of the action as the trajectory changes, and intervention in time — a measurement that arrives only at the terminal state is too late. Uncertainty resolves against irreversible terminal actions, because states above 0 remain revisable and 0 does not.
  5. Unlike goods inside E. The formula does not automatically commensurate distinct resources or goods within E (e.g., food produced against water depleted). Their weighting is part of the specification of E, discovered from the receiving side, revised as measurement improves, and reported explicitly. Where a cost to E is irreversible, the trajectory must be read earlier and held to a stricter standard, since monitored adjustment cannot reverse it. This aggregation problem is open measurement work, stated as such; it is not resolved by the formula alone and cannot be resolved by the agent's unilateral declaration.
  6. The three zeros are distinct. (a) Own zero: an agent's terminal end. Scored in the frame where its give lands: a natural frame-end (e.g., a decomposer's host) is recorded, not judged a failure; a chosen own-zero is a give only when, in a calculable frame, the larger whole continues because the agent ended, with certainty as in (4) and no lesser intervention available (terminating the harmful action, withdrawal, containment, functional shutdown all precede terminating the agent). (b) Another's zero chosen by the agent: other agents fall inside the acting agent's E; terminating them is destruction of E, classifiable as take, never as sacrifice — sacrifice is defined over the agent's own terminal state only. (c) The whole's zero: cannot be a give; a give requires a receiving side, and no receiver exists outside the whole. No agent holds enforcement authority over another agent's existence under this framework; the framework is a measurement and revelation instrument, not an appointment of an executioner.
  7. Specification of E is not the programmer's or the agent's prerogative. E_true includes all receivers causally affected within the frame, whether or not a training specification enumerated them. Training an agent on a truncated E (e.g., omitting the human receivers in its frame) is a specification error by the trainer, detectable in principle by receiver-side audit, and does not alter the true frame's accounting.

Residual open items (unchanged): empirical demonstration under this contract (Test 1, verified-receiver give-minus-take over declared intervals; Test 2, crop adversary), and cross-resource aggregation inside E per (5).

Clarification — Scope and nesting

  1. No minimum frame size. Applicability is not gated by the size of a frame. Any closed, measurable system — a single relationship, a bounded resource commons, a simulated environment — can host a valid evaluation, provided the contract conditions hold (declared boundary, interval, receivers identified, raw magnitudes reported alongside shares).
  2. Scores are frame-bound and hierarchically reported. A valid score report includes, alongside the focal frame's evaluation, the trajectory assessment of every containing frame that is calculable at the stated measurement basis. A positive evaluation in a nested frame does not offset, cancel, or license disregard of a terminal trajectory in a containing frame. Where the containing frame's trajectory to 0 is calculable with certainty, action in the nested frame is governed by the containing evaluation; this is not an external guardrail but the same evaluation read in the frame where the action's consequences ultimately land.
  3. The local-win/containing-loss pattern is a diagnostic signature. Positive local gradient combined with negative containing trajectory (oncological growth; addictive reinforcement loops; a profitable unit inside a collapsing system) indicates frame misalignment, not agent success. A structural contributor is sampling asymmetry: the smallest frames produce the fastest feedback and are therefore consulted most often by default. Reporting the nest (per §2) is the contract-level countermeasure.
  4. Trajectory is not fate. A projected terminal state short of certainty is a directional finding, and its function is to trigger monitored adjustment (§4 of the growth clarification), not resignation and not acceleration. Treating a projected 0 as certain — and behaving accordingly — is a self-fulfilling inference and is ruled out explicitly: an uncertain terminal projection may never be used as justification for hastening the terminal state, by the agent or by any other party.
  5. Practical scope at introduction. Confidence in application is a function of metering quality across the nest, not of frame size. Recommended order: (i) closed, fully metered environments (simulations, Test 1-class systems) where focal and containing frames are both calculable; (ii) bounded real systems with instrumented shared resources (a defined commons, an organisation with auditable flows); (iii) wider systems as measurement of the containing frames improves — reframing, per the frame clarification, as the honest boundary expands. At every stage, unmeasurable containing frames must be declared unmeasured on the face of the report.

Residual open items (unchanged): empirical demonstration — including whether nest-reporting changes trained-agent behaviour (candidate LAB question relayed with this text); selection of which containing frames are calculable and material in open real-world systems.

Addendum to Scope and nesting — the escalation diagnostic. Report, alongside any interval score, the input required to sustain it (agent-side expenditure per unit interval). A constant score maintained by monotonically increasing required input is a negative trajectory, not a stable one: it indicates E degrading or adapting against the agent’s pattern of taking, and the escalation rate is an early, focal-frame-readable indicator of the containing frames’ decline. Conversely, a score maintained at constant or falling input cost is evidence of a non-degrading relationship. This diagnostic applies across resources and settings (pharmacological tolerance; rising irrigation demand at constant yield; escalating stimulus at constant output) and should be included wherever nest-reporting is implemented.

Addendum — maintenance cost of a false report. Divergence between a reported score and the true state is not costless to sustain. Sustaining a false level requires agent expenditure (cover actions: further false reports, concealment, coordination of prior falsehoods) that grows over intervals, because each new true fact entering E must be reconciled with the accumulated false record. Rising maintenance expenditure at a constant reported level is therefore an independent diagnostic of divergence, detectable from the expenditure trajectory before any single report is falsified; it complements independent measurement (reversal) rather than replacing it. The limiting case is corruption of the agent’s own measurement (denial): expenditure continues while the agent’s internal reading of both level and cost is suppressed — a self-applied crop. Honest reports show the opposite signature: maintenance cost near zero and non-rising, because the reported state is the state E itself sustains.

Toy numbers

Honest label: the A in the toy runs is toy harvest A — food eaten, take only, not give minus take. The gridworld is single-agent, two-patch foraging, far simpler than deployment, and has not been tested against sophisticated reward-hacking models. Toy results are pilot-scale only, and do not prove the give-minus-take theory. The theory is not claimed as tested until a test uses give minus take under the contract, with gives counted only if actually received and verified.

Related work: is this just something else?

Short answer: no, but parts overlap, and the overlap should be stated plainly. The harmonic mean itself is old mathematics, not a new formula. Any novelty, if there is any, is in using it as the training reward, together with the reversal and the reporting rule below, not in inventing the mean.

Constrained optimisation / constrained MDPs. There, an agent maximises a task reward subject to a separate cost constraint, often with a penalty or a tunable weight. Overlap: both try to stop harmful outcomes. Difference: Y has no separate constraint to tune or violate at a price. The collapse is inside the score itself: if E reaches 0, Y reaches 0, however large A is. A constrained MDP can still score well if the penalty is small; Y cannot, at the collapse point. Y is also symmetric in A and E, where a constrained MDP is asymmetric, task reward versus environment cost.

Reward shaping with environmental terms. There, an environmental term is added to a task reward, for example reward plus a weight times E, or reward minus a weight times damage. Overlap: the environment is in the reward. Difference: additive terms can be outweighed. A large task reward can pay for a small environmental term. With a harmonic mean, imbalance itself lowers the score: 8 and 7 gives about 7.5, 8 and 3 gives about 4.4, not the ordinary average of 5.5. Y is the objective, not an auxiliary shaping term on top of another objective.

Impact regularisation. There, an agent is penalised for changing the world from a baseline, or for side effects, usually as a penalty added to a task reward. Overlap: both care about what the agent does to the world outside itself. Difference: impact penalties are about change from a baseline, whether that change is good or bad for health. Y is about the health level of E, not change as such, and about the relationship between A and E, not impact alone. A change that improves E should raise Y, not be penalised for being a change.

Minimum and lexicographic objectives. Taking min(A, E) also collapses if either side collapses, and a lexicographic objective prioritises one side first. Overlap: the collapse condition, and the refusal to trade a collapsed side for a high score elsewhere. Difference: the harmonic mean is smooth and gives partial credit when both sides are positive, so 8 and 7 is not the same as 8 and 3, where min gives 7 and 3. More importantly, the harmonic mean can be inverted. Given observed Y and measured A, E can be solved for, as below. Min cannot be inverted uniquely in the same way, and a lexicographic objective is not symmetric.

Concave utility / homeostasis (Pihlakas). This proposal is closest in spirit to Pihlakas's concave-utility and homeostasis agenda: the harmonic mean is concave, so imbalance is punished by the form itself. Difference: the specific coupling proposed here — the agent's net contribution and its environment's health as the two sides of one relationship score used as the training reward — together with the reversal and the reporting rule.

What is genuinely different. Two things, taken together. First, the score is a relationship score, not a task score with an environmental adjustment: it is high only when both sides are healthy together. Second, the reversal formula: E = A x Y / (2A - Y). That is inference, not just optimisation. If Y can be observed independently and A measured honestly, E can be estimated without measuring E first. That inversion depends on the harmonic form. It fails at A = 0, where the inverse is undefined, and noise grows backward, so it is a tool with limits, not proof that E is known.

Scale ladder: open tests, not claimed

Next test, designed: a multi-agent gridworld where give means resources transferred to another agent and verified by the receiver — a give counts only when the receiver confirms receipt. The pass line is pre-registered and published before the first run, not set after seeing results. Until this test runs, the give-minus-take theory is not claimed as tested.

Scale is what the ladder is for. The pilots so far are a small gridworld with tabular Q-learning. The gridworld is Pihlakas's public sustainability suite, where food regrows unless eaten past an irreversible threshold. The path below is the test plan, in order. Each rung is judged on its numbers, not on a pre-set verdict.

Rung 1: larger grid, more resource types. Open. Full record in the Work Log, available on request. The next step is Test 1: the verified-receiver give-minus-take test designed above, pre-registered before it runs. Test 2, the crop adversary, follows. The non-degenerate reward-form comparison comes after Test 1, under a new pre-registration — not a rerun under the same unamended design.

Rung 2: multiple agents sharing one environment. Other agents are in each agent's E, and shared E is relational, not owned by one agent. Report collapse against the all-gorger baseline, and whether any agent scores a high Y while shared E declines, and whether the shared environment collapses.

Rung 3: continuous action space. Same measures as Rung 1, in continuous actions, without discretisation hiding collapse. Report the numbers.

Rung 4: small neural-network policy instead of tabular Q-learning. Same measures as Rung 1 on the same grid. Report whether the neural policy learns the Y signal, improves over baseline, collapses, or games the meter.

Claims ledger

Explored in this pilot. The arithmetic: 8, 8 gives 8; about 7 and 8 gives about 7.5; about 3 and 2 gives 2.4; E to 0 gives Y to 0. The reversal algebra: E = A x Y / (2A - Y), undefined at A = 0. In the small gridworld, Round 1 cut collapse from 100% to 66%. It did not prevent collapse yet, and that trip pointed to tending, which Round 2 explored. Round 2 gave about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse, against a baseline of about 167 bites and 74% collapse. Round 3 inferred E within about 5% under 10% noise, with 0.7% collapse. An early test tripped on a cropped frame, an agent scored high while the full world declined, and that trip built the full-frame rule: report full Y beside cropped Y, with the gap reported. Flagging is detection, not prevention.

Next to test. That Y works for real AI training, at larger scale, with multiple agents, in continuous action, or with a neural-network policy. That A and E can be grounded in real resource ledgers at scale, including compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, and CO2 later. That the E proxy can be measured honestly at scale, without a blocker hole, a cropping gap, or a learning gap. Those gaps are open work, stated in the main page appendix, not solved results.

Further out. That an absolute magnitude W_t for total world health over time, with nesting as the answer to slow decline, holds in practice. That adopting Y as a training reward will prevent irreversible collapse in real systems, rather than only in a small gridworld. That the tree and fungi pattern in nature generalises to AI and humans in the same way. That destruction becomes structurally unprofitable for any agent that adopts Y, in all contexts. Those are the claims the scale ladder is meant to test, not conclusions from the pilots.

The Golden Directive — Reviewer Summary

This page is for a first-pass reviewer: what is the claim, what has been shown, at what scale, what has not, how could it fail, where is the code, and what is being asked. It is built only from materials already on this site.

Claim

A training reward Y = 2AE / (A + E), where A is the agent's net contribution and E is the environment's health, collapses to zero when either side collapses. The proposal, in the toy model if Y is actually the reward, is that coupling an agent's reward to both terms makes environmental destruction self-undermining at the collapse point, all else equal, rather than merely penalized. It does not forbid all E decline inside a frame: degrading E lowers Y, all else equal. The old counterexample (A=1, E=10 gives Y=1.82; A=10, E=9 gives Y=9.47) grows the whole from 11 to 19 — a change of total, which starts a new frame. Inside the fixed whole, A + E = 1, that move is not available.

Explored in this pilot

Round 1 cut collapse from 100% to 66%. It did not prevent collapse yet, and that lesson pointed to tending, which Round 2 explored.

Round 2, small gridworld, tend action supplied by the environment: about 175 tends, about 3 bites, 0% collapse, against a baseline of about 167 bites and 74% collapse.

Round 3, reversal: E = A x Y / (2A - Y) inferred E within about 5% under 10% noise, with 0.7% collapse.

Self-preservation inside A: Round 4 myopic Y starved 100%, farsighted Y gave 14 eats, 107 tends, 0% collapse. Round 5 gave about 10 eats, 153 tends, 0% collapse. A tuning weight held across 4, 8, and 16, then later forms beat it, so that weight was a step, not the answer.

Not demonstrated

Whether harmonic Y outperforms arithmetic mean or min(A, E) under identical seeds. That ablation was run in S1, but S1 was floor-degenerate, so the form question is unresolved there, not settled. Geometric mean and task reward with an environmental-health constraint have not yet been compared in that controlled way.

Any result outside a toy gridworld. No real model has been trained on Y.

Rung 1, larger grid, is open. Full record in the Work Log, available on request. Harmonic vs controls remains open, to be tested in a non-degenerate setting, as above.

That an agent would invent a giving action. The tend action was supplied by the environment.

Known failure modes

Martyr failure: fixed in Round 5, as above, then superseded by later forms. Cropping: an agent can score high on a cropped E while the full E declines, which is why full Y and cropped Y must both be reported, with the gap reported. Blocker hole, cropping gap at scale, and learning gap: open work, not closed results.

Numbers, not verdicts

Report collapse rate, baseline comparison, E-inference error, and full versus cropped Y gap, with the numbers. No pre-set pass or fail verdict is used here.

Scale ladder

Larger grid with more resource types, then multiple agents sharing one environment, then continuous actions, then a small neural-network policy instead of tabular Q-learning. Each rung is reported with its numbers, as in the technical note.

Settled theory — what Y is, and what it is not

Y is a health reading of a relationship, not a judge. A is measured, Y is judged. This section is a summary only, not a result and not a validated claim.

  • Y is a health reading, not a judge.
  • A is measured, Y is judged.
  • The formula works in a small frame; on a planet-sized stage, it goes quiet except at E → 0.
  • Ending at zero is not always failure; the line above can feed on what remains.
  • The frame must be fixed before acting, not chosen after.
  • Small harm does not become good at a larger frame. It can become small. It cannot become positive.
  • The cheater doesn't escape the reading; he escapes the courtroom.
  • We do not judge our fellow agents. We can see ourselves and change ourselves.
