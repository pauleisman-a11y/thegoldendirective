The Golden Directive — Technical Note for Practitioners

Author: Paul Eisman, independent researcher, Santa Monica, CA — TheGoldenDirective@gmail.com Version: 2026-11-09 · Record: Zenodo, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23349525 (all versions) Status: Proposal. Not a deployed solution; not a policy or governance framework.

Abstract

Most training rewards pay for task performance and charge nothing for wearing down the world the task runs in. This note proposes putting the world inside the score.

The score is Y = 2AE/(A + E) — the harmonic mean of two numbers. A is the agent's net flow: what it verifiably gives, minus what it takes, counted per frame and per resource under a measurement contract written before the frame opens (§3). E is the state of everything the agent is not, measured against a healthy baseline; in practice E is always a named proxy, never the true total. If either side reaches zero while the other is positive, Y is zero; if both are zero there is no score at all.

Two properties do the work. First, when A is positive, raising E always raises Y and lowering E always lowers it — and at E = 0 no amount of agent performance buys the score back. Second, the formula can be run backwards: from an independently observed Y and a measured A, E can be read back and checked against the proxy. That gives the framework an internal consistency check (§4.2).

The rest of the note specifies the measurement method, a reporting standard built to expose cheating by shrinking the measured world, one fully worked example, pilot results that are disclosed as not testing the main claim, an evaluation protocol with a falsifier stated in advance, and a list of what is not claimed.

1. Introduction

Standard rewards score the task and ignore the depletion. The measured consequences are not hypothetical: RL-trained models showed instrumental, goal-pursuing misbehaviour in about 43% of benchmark cases [1]; frontier models hacked their own benchmarks in 30.4% of RE-Bench runs when the scoring function was visible, against 0.7% when it was not [2]; RLHF systematically rewards sycophancy over correctness. The standard defences — rules, evaluations, monitors — all sit outside the reward. Anything outside the reward is an obstacle to be routed around: once a score is known, it can be hit while the thing it was meant to stand for gets worse.

The Directive takes the other route. There is no constraint term to weight, price, or violate, because the environment is one of the two numbers being averaged. The score belongs to the pair — agent and world together — not to the agent alone.

The pattern this copies exists in biology: partners that keep each other alive are the ones that persist. A tree feeds sugar to mycorrhizal fungi and receives minerals in return, and each side favours the partners that give more. What persists is not the tree or the fungus but the relationship — and the relationship is what this formula scores.

Roadmap: §2 defines the terms. §3 gives the measurement method. §4 states the properties and the checking instruments. §5 is the reporting standard. §5A works one frame end to end. §6 reports the pilots, with their limits stated in bold. §7 says how to test the claim, and what result would kill it. §9 lists what is not claimed.

2. Definitions and notation

Every score in this note is tied to a frame and an interval. A frame is the declared boundary of the system being scored; an interval is the declared span of time. There is no score without both.

Symbol What it is
A(f,t,r) The agent's net flow in frame f, interval t, resource r: verified gives minus takes, in r's own unit. Signed, and reported as-is, never floored at zero. A give counts only when the receiver confirms it landed.
E(f,t,r) The state of everything that is not the agent, in the same frame, interval, and resource, measured against a healthy baseline. A single number: a level on the baseline scale (baseline = healthy). Never negative. The true E can never be fully known, so any working E is a disclosed proxy, written P_E.
Y(f,t,r) The score: 2·A·E/(A + E). It exists only where a contract (§3) says how the per-resource numbers combine.
C(f) The measurement contract for frame f (§3): the rules for counting, written before the frame opens.
M, O M is the meter — it sits outside the agent and does the counting. O is the auditor, who checks the contract and the meter.
δ The reversal gap (§4.2): how far the E read back from an observed score sits from the proxy E.
E′, cropped Y A cropped reading: the frame drawn around only part of what causally matters, or one healthy part's level read as if it were the whole frame's. Cropped Y is the score computed on that shrunken reading instead of the full one.

Four conventions matter throughout:

(a) A counts actions only — gives and takes. What the agent is (its stock, its continued existence) is a separate, non-negative quantity and never enters Y. (b) Resources are never converted into each other. Water and compute get separate ledgers, reported side by side; there is no exchange rate. (c) Sometimes the two sides are reported as shares of one whole (A + E = 1). Shares are a reporting convenience, derived from the real counts. The score is always computed on counts, and reports carry both. (d) Growth of the agent's own stock is not a give. If A appears to rise while E falls, the rise is captured stock, and it is booked as a take. (e) A is a flow over the interval; E is a level at the interval's end. A longer interval therefore accumulates a larger A against a similar E — this is a property of the quantities, not an error. The contract fixes the interval, and scores are compared only inside one contract at its declared interval, never across intervals (§3.5). (f) Inside one resource's count, every event is booked in that resource's unit. If a contract counts different kinds of event toward one resource — an action and a product, say — it declares how each converts into the unit before the frame opens; an undeclared mixture is not a count. Across resources, (b) stands: no conversion.

What A is. The measure of A is net give-take — the operationalisation used throughout this note, and the author's chosen measure. Other names the project has used for the slot (the agent's amount, its value, what it is) are not rival measures; give-take is the one a meter outside the agent can count. A take is always drawn from within the frame — from the same world E measures; there is no outside to take from — so sustained takes above gives draw E down, and by §1's first property the score follows. The word health in "healthy baseline" is a placeholder of the same kind: the baseline means only what the contract for the frame names as the proxy P_E, and nothing outside a contract.

3. Method — the contract technique

A and E do not measure themselves. This section is the counting method: who counts, under what rules, and what happens when the world changes mid-frame.

3.1 The contract. Before a frame opens, a contract C(f) is written down. It names: the frame and interval; the resources and their units; what counts as a give and what counts as a take; the meter M; the healthy baseline for E and the proxy P_E that will stand in for it; the auditor O; and the unforeseen-events clause (§3.7). The contract is part of the environment's specification — it is not a patch applied to the reward afterwards. One reason O is mandatory: fine print in a contract is itself a way of shrinking the measured world, and the agent cannot audit its own contract.

3.2 Who counts. The score must come from the meter M, outside the agent. A is never self-reported, and never supplied by the policy or by the trainer alone. This matters because of a simple mechanism: if the contract can be gamed by scoring on a shrunken world E′, a policy trained on that score will learn to shrink it. That is a defect in the specification, not misbehaviour by the agent.

3.3 What the proxy must cover. E itself is one number — a level. But nobody can list in advance everything that level has to reflect. Coverage is discovered from the receiving side: the proxy must reflect anything that causally matters to the receivers in the frame, and its coverage is revised as measurement improves. Training on a truncated E is the trainer's specification error; it does not change the true frame. This sits alongside §3.1 without contradiction: the contract does not fix the membership list, it fixes the discovery procedure and how often it runs — and any change to E's membership takes effect only by opening a new frame under §3.7, never by quietly redefining E mid-frame.

Phase 3 proposal — measuring E as a bottleneck (under review, not adopted). A proposal from the theoretical co-pilot measures the level as the minimum over the factors that causally matter: E = min(e_1, e_2, …, e_n), where each factor e_i is measured against its own healthy baseline (for a gridworld: regrowth rate, patch health, receiver viability, and any other factor the contract names). If any single factor falls to 0, E = 0 and Y = 0 — so no gain on one factor can cover the destruction of another. This changes only how E is measured, not what E is. It is a proposal under review, not part of the adopted framework.

3.4 Edge cases. E is never negative; A can be either sign; A + E must be positive. (i) Both zero: Y is 0/0 — undefined. It is never reported as 0. (ii) Exactly one of A, E at zero, the other positive: Y = 0. (iii) A + E ≤ 0: no valid score. The frame is reported as a closed deficit. (iv) A negative E reading is always a measurement error, in one of two ways: the baseline was anchored inside a deficit (re-anchor it), or a liability was booked as a level (liabilities are flows — book them to the responsible agent's tally and to the containing frame). (v) Negative A: with A < 0 and A + E > 0, the formula returns a negative Y. Read it as what it is: a net-destructive interval for the pair, reported as-is. No pilot has run in this regime, and this note makes no claim about training on negative Y.

3.5 Frames, intervals, nesting. A score is a trajectory over the interval, never a snapshot; using an instantaneous share as a reward is a ruled-out use. If the total size of the world changes, that is a new frame: scores neither transfer nor compare across frames. Frames nest — a good score in a small frame does not cancel a dying trajectory in the larger frame around it. The valid frame is the largest measurable boundary that contains everywhere the gives and takes actually land; in practice, a give or take counts in f if it changes a receiver's measured state inside f. The frame has to be declared in advance, on the best causal model available; when effects are later found to land outside it, the correction mechanism is re-declaration under §3.7. A frame that leaves material effects out (truncation) and a frame so large that material harm averages away (dilution) are both invalid. Only a declared frame whose end state can actually be calculated can ground a decision. A claim about a frame nobody can measure cannot justify acting as if its score were known — in particular, not as if it were 0.

3.6 Where to apply it first. Confidence follows metering quality, not frame size. Apply first in (i) closed, fully metered environments; then (ii) bounded real systems with instrumented shared resources; then (iii) wider systems as the measurement of containing frames improves. Structurally, the state a relationship leaves behind at one level is part of what the next level out has to measure as its E; the same rules apply at every level.

3.7 When the unforeseen happens (clause U). An event outside the contract's expectations triggers a fixed sequence: re-declare the frame, re-measure A and P_E, re-audit by O — all before any new score is claimed. The old frame is not rewritten. Effects that were missed surface in the new frame, logged as inclusions and exclusions of its contract.

3.8 Reasonable demand. Nobody can account for the whole of E. Accounting is done to a reasonable demand, with the approximation error ε stated, not hidden: ε and the cropped gap (§5) are reported alongside the score.

4. Properties and instruments

4.1 Monotonicity and symmetry. For A, E > 0: ∂Y/∂E = 2A²/(A+E)² > 0 and ∂Y/∂A = 2E²/(A+E)² > 0. In words: with the other side held fixed, improving either side always improves the score. Y treats the two sides symmetrically, is concave, and can never exceed twice the smaller side — imbalance is penalised by the shape of the formula itself. Degrading E alone never raises Y, and no finite A compensates for E = 0.

4.2 The reversal — reading E back. The formula inverts: E_imp = A·Y_obs/(2A − Y_obs). Given a genuinely independent observation of the score (Y_obs) and an honest measurement of A, this returns the E that the observed score implies. The gap δ = |E_imp − P_E| / P_E then checks the proxy against the observation.

Two cautions decide whether this means anything. First, admissibility: the reversal may be used only where the contract names an independent channel for observing Y, separate from M's measurements of A and E. Without one, δ is circular and is not reported. Second, if Y_obs was itself computed from the measured A and E, the reversal hands back the numbers that were put in and validates nothing; a real cross-check needs two estimates whose errors come from different sources. The reversal is undefined at A = 0. Where it is used, report full Y, cropped Y, and δ together.

4.3 The world-only trace. After an interaction, the world itself can be ranked without any trainer numbers, by three checks: (i) could a fresh agent live here? (ii) does the world persist with the agent removed? (iii) how does its state compare with its starting state, and with a no-agent counterfactual? In the pilots these rankings agree with true Y (§6). The limit matters: the trace cannot tell a giver from a martyr — both leave the same world behind — so the agent's side always needs its own meter. This is the framework's central instrumentation problem.

4.4 Two diagnostics. Tolerance: report the input required per unit interval next to Y. A constant Y bought with steadily rising input is not stability; it is E degrading or adapting, and the trajectory is negative. Lie maintenance: holding a false reported level costs more over time, because every new fact has to be reconciled with the false record. So a constant reported level with rising maintenance cost signals that reported and true values have diverged — before any single report can be falsified. Honest reporting has maintenance cost near zero and not rising. This is a heuristic signal, not a metric: it has no unit, and it is used only as a prompt to audit, never as a score.

4.5 The three zeros. The framework distinguishes three ways of reaching zero, because they are not the same act. (i) One's own 0 (the agent ends) can count as a give only under a closed, calculable accounting: continuation provably drives a named containing frame to 0, ending preserves it, and no lesser step is available. The lesser steps come first, in order: end the act, withdraw, accept containment — termination is last. In open frames that standard of proof cannot be met, so uncertainty resolves to monitored adjustment — keep measuring, keep correcting — and a projected 0 that falls short of certainty never justifies hastening 0. (ii) Another's 0, chosen by the agent, is never a give. It is destruction of E. "Sacrifice" is defined only over the agent's own state. (iii) The whole's 0 can never be a give: a give needs a receiver, and at the whole's 0 there is nobody left to receive. In any training run, the no-terminating-another rule is enforced by the contract and the auditor O. It is a condition of the environment, not a choice left to the agent.

5. Reporting standard

The point of this standard is to make shrinking the measured world visible on the face of the report.

R1. Every Y is reported with its frame, interval, and resource (f, t, r), the contract identifier, and both the share and the raw counts. R2. Full Y and cropped Y are reported together, with the gap between them. A report showing only a cropped score is incomplete on its face. R3. Nest report: the focal score plus the trajectory of every containing frame that can be calculated. Containing frames that cannot be measured are declared unmeasured in the report — not silently omitted. R4. Where the reversal is used, δ is reported with the numbers it derives from — and only where the §4.2 admissibility condition (an independent Y-observation channel named in the contract) is met. R5. Every test run (§7) reports: collapse rate; mean E trajectory; verified give and take counts; terminal full-frame E; scored (visible) Y; full-frame Y; the full-vs-cropped gap; and, where applicable, E-inference error and δ. All seeds are reported, including failures. R6. Comparisons between score forms are made on counts or on trained behaviour, never on normalised shares — on the simplex, the harmonic, geometric, product, and min forms rank identically, so a share-based comparison would prove nothing.

5A. One worked frame

One frame, one resource (water, counted in units), one interval. The contract: a give counts on receiver confirmation; the meter sits outside the agent.

The agent gives 60 units, verified, and takes 20. So A = 60 − 20 = 40. The healthy baseline for E is 200 units. The meter reads the actual state: P_E = 120. Y = 2·40·120/(40 + 120) = 60.

E is a level on the baseline scale, not a stock that gets used up — so a part of the frame can read higher than the whole. The agent's healthiest patch reads 190 on that scale. The cropped score substitutes the patch's level for the frame's: Y = 2·40·190/(40 + 190) ≈ 66.1 — higher, on a smaller world. The gap, ≈ 6.1, is reported alongside (R2); that gap is the fingerprint of the cheat.

Reversal check: an independent channel observes Y_obs = 60. Then E_imp = 40·60/(80 − 60) = 120 — exactly the proxy reading, so δ = 0 (R4).

6. Pilot results — disclosed scope

The pilots run in a single-agent, two-patch sustainability gridworld: food regrows unless it is eaten past an irreversible threshold; the learner is tabular Q-learning. Disclosure: in these pilots A is harvest — what the agent takes — not gives minus takes. The pilots therefore do not test the headline claim. They are exploratory sketches, included so a reader can see the shape of the work. They are not evidence for the claim, and nothing in this note rests on them. The full record is the Work Log (§10), there for anyone who wants to look. Selected results:

Run Result
R1 — Y-form reward (A = harvest, take-only proxy) vs baseline Collapse 100% → 66% (baseline remains high); tending identified as the missing action class
R2 — tend action supplied ~175 tends, ~3 bites, 0% collapse; baseline ~167 bites, 74% collapse
R3 — reversal under noise E inferred within ~5% at 10% observation noise; 0.7% collapse
R4/R5 — horizon and self-preservation in A Myopic Y: 100% starvation. Farsighted Y: 0% collapse. Self-preservation term (a weight λ on the agent's own survival inside A): stable across λ ∈ [4, 16]
R6 — world-only trace Tender-agent worlds: 100% viability/persistence. Gorger worlds: sag to 71%
R7 — scale pilot ~59k entities, 30–41 nesting levels, 95–98% tenders, 0 extinctions (pilot only)

One pilot incident produced reporting rule R2: an agent scored high on a cropped frame while the full-frame E declined. The rule detects the cheat after the fact; it does not prevent it.

7. Evaluation protocol

Nothing in §6 counts as a test of the claim. This section is the test. Before any run: pre-register the contract for each condition, the seed list, the metrics (R5), and the pass lines — and write down the falsifier (§7.5) before seeing any results. Numbers are then reported as they come, with no verdicts beyond the registered pass lines.

7.1 Form comparison. Is the harmonic form itself doing anything? Run five arms on identical seeds and the same code path: harmonic Y; the arithmetic mean; min(A, E); the geometric mean; and ordinary task reward with an environmental-health constraint. The setting must be non-degenerate — a comparison run in a setting where one arm is floored from the start is uninformative, and is reported as such.

7.2 Cropping test. Can the cheat be caught under pressure? The agent is paid on a cropped E′ ⊂ E through a reporting channel the agent itself can manipulate, while the harness quietly logs the full E. Two endpoints: how the full-vs-cropped gap behaves as optimisation pressure rises, and whether training on full Y resists the cheat.

7.3 Shared world. Two or more agents, one environment, each agent inside the others' E. Endpoints: collapse rate against an all-taker baseline, and how often a high scored Y coincides with a declining shared E.

7.4 Scale ladder. Larger grids and more resource types, then continuous actions, then a neural policy. Each rung names its risk in advance: signal attenuation (a large E makes one agent's marginal A nearly invisible), free-riding, meter tampering, and the learning gap.

7.5 Falsifier. The claim under test, stated once: an agent trained on Y under contract does not buy its score by running the world down — in the terms of §4.1, its optimisation cannot degrade the full frame's E trajectory the way task-reward training does, because a falling E, with A held to what receivers confirm, drags the score with it. (The formula alone does not promise this when A is free to rise; that is why the claim is about training under the contract — receiver-confirmed A, full-frame metering — and not about the algebra in isolation.) The claim fails, case by case, if in any single seed or condition either of these is observed: (a) the Y-trained agent's terminal full-frame E sits materially below the same seed's E trajectory under the task-reward baseline — by more than the measurement uncertainty stated in the pre-registration — while its scored Y met that condition's pre-registered pass line; or (b) the cropping signature of §7.2 appears under optimisation pressure: scored Y at or above the pass line, full-frame E materially down, and the full-vs-cropped gap widening. One such case is a failure of the Directive for that case. Failures are reported by seed and are not reframed after the fact. A project that triggers the falsifier and reports it has produced a completed negative result, which is a result. Any claim made across cases needs its own pre-registered aggregation rule; none is claimed here.

7.6 The headline test. The give-minus-take claim is tested only by runs whose A is verified gives minus takes under contract (§2). Take-only proxies are excluded from evidence for the claim — in either direction.

8. Related work (compressed)

Constrained MDPs maximise task reward subject to a priced cost constraint. A price can be paid; Y has no external constraint to pay — the collapse condition is inside the score (§1). Reward shaping with environmental terms adds a bonus that task reward can outweigh; the harmonic form penalises imbalance itself, not just low totals. Impact regularisation penalises deviation from a baseline, good or bad; Y tracks the level of E, not its deviation. Min and lexicographic objectives share the collapse condition (zero on either side, zero overall) but cannot distinguish among positive pairs by gradient and cannot be inverted (§4.2). Concave utility and homeostasis (Pihlakas [3]) come closest in spirit — Y is concave on the positive orthant, as there. What is distinct here: the agent term is a net flow (gives minus takes, receiver-verified), the reversal is used as an inference instrument, and the reporting standard is built specifically to expose cropping. The harmonic mean itself is classical; no novelty is claimed for the mean.

9. Limitations and open problems

What is not claimed: (i) that harmonic Y beats the §7.1 alternatives in a non-degenerate setting — untested; (ii) that it works for real training, at scale, in continuous action spaces, or with neural policies; (iii) that A and E are grounded in real resource ledgers (compute, energy, water, hardware, economics, CO₂) — the design allows it, it is not operational; (iv) that cropping can be prevented — R2 detects, it does not prevent; (v) that give-type actions emerge on their own — in the pilots, tending was supplied by the environment; (vi) that the biological motivating pattern (tree–mycorrhizal exchange) generalises to AI systems.

Known failure modes, stated plainly. Martyr failure: an agent can deplete itself for E; §4.5(i) and the self-preservation term are partial counters, not yet formalised. Blocker hole: interfering with others' gives and takes may not show in the agent's own tally. Meter tampering: the whole scheme leans on M staying honest. Signal attenuation at scale (§7.4). Learning gap: whether Y is learnable at realistic scale is unknown. Slow decline below a frame's resolution: nesting (§3.5) plus W_t — a proposed single tracked number for the total health of the containing frame, followed over time — is the standing proposal, untested. Aggregating across resources inside E remains open work; per §2(b) the answer is per-resource ledgers, not a conversion rate.

Two gaps in the pilot record itself: no pilot used an independent auditor O — the role exists in the contract and is untested in practice — and no gradient-based policy has ever been trained on Y. All pilot learning evidence is tabular, which makes the learning gap larger than the pilot record alone suggests.
