Chapter I

The Relationship

Formula · examples · first experiments

Humans cannot survive what they destroy. AI cannot survive what it destroys. Anything living inside a system cannot survive destroying it.

Y=2(A × E)A + E

You = twice (what you give × everything you are not), divided by their sum.

9, 9 → 9Both healthy.
9, 1 → 1.8One side is failing.
100, 0.1 → 0.2Brilliance cannot hide collapse.
A

Net contribution after taking, as a nonnegative contribution-health score — negative raw give-minus-take values are floored at zero before entering the mean. A is like calories — input versus output. You can't run a deficit forever and score well. Net of calories: one number — give minus take. The proposed measure uses attributed changes in E, never the agent’s own account; independent A attribution now exists in the small gridworld, where the richer trace reads tender, martyr, and gorger from actions alone.

E

The health of everything not-A, against a healthy baseline.

Y

The relationship score. If either side reaches zero, Y reaches zero.

Tree + mycorrhizal fungi

Worked example

E1, E2, and E3 are each scored from 0 to 10 against a healthy baseline. Combined E is the arithmetic mean of the sub-scores.

E1Life sustained · 0-10
E2Resources remaining · 0-10
E3Expected longevity · 0-10

Healthy

E1 = 8 · E2 = 6 · E3 = 7 · Combined E ≈ 7 · A = 8Y = 2(8 × 7)/(8 + 7) = 112/15 ≈ 7.5

Parasitic

E1 = 3 · E2 = 4 · E3 = 2 · Combined E ≈ 3 · A = 2Y = 2(2 × 3)/(2 + 3) = 12/5 = 2.4

Dead tree

E → 0 therefore Y → 0

Real science: Kiers et al., Science 2011, “Reciprocal Rewards Stabilize Cooperation in the Mycorrhizal Symbiosis” - Plants feed the fungi that feed them most, and give less to the stingy ones. The fungi bargain back the same way. Nobody gets a free ride.

The roles reverse cleanly. Take the human as the agent and the AI sits inside E. It works AI-to-AI the same way.

Now reverse the formula and ask what the relationship can reveal about E.

The frame must be full, not cropped. A score on a cropped frame is not the relationship.

First experiments

Tested, not just claimed. Three small gridworld rounds tested whether coupling reward to E changes behavior - and whether an agent can recover E without ever seeing it.

Round 1 - does coupling the reward to E prevent collapse?

A learning agent foraged in a small world where food regrows - unless eaten past the point of no return. Two rewards were tested: plain “eat more” versus Y.

The plain myopic agent ate the world dead every time: 100% collapse.

The Y myopic agent: 66% collapse, 4× the food. Better - but coupling alone softened collapse without preventing it.

Farsighted agents sustained under both rewards. Y didn't hurt good behavior.

Round 2 - what would it give?

The agent got a new action: TEND the land (+1 food availability), and A was redefined as give minus take - so only net giving scores, and eating pays zero.

The Y myopic agent tended ~175 times per episode, ate ~3 bites, and ended at full health every time: 0% collapse.

The baseline myopic agent ate ~167 bites per episode and collapsed 74% of the time.

It took a little sugar and delivered a lot of minerals - the fungi, in code.

Round 3 - the reversal test

Agents never observed E. Starting from a deliberately wrong guess, they inferred it only through E = AY/(2A-Y), under 10% reward noise. A control arm saw true E; both arms received the same noisy reward.

  • Inferred E converged to within ~5% of true E within the first episodes.
  • Bootstrap myopic: ~180 tends, ~2 bites, 0.7% collapse, and full world health - indistinguishable from control.
  • Bootstrap farsighted: ~108 tends, ~8.6 food, 2.3% collapse.
  • Bootstrap threshold-E: 0.3% collapse.

The agent-side E-meter was unnecessary: the information it needed about E was already in (A, Y). Give nothing, learn nothing - when A = 0, the inverse is undefined.

The Reversal

Inference · boundaries · nesting

We may know E after Y.

E=AY2A − Y

The first version measured E, then calculated the relationship. The reversal lets the relationship happen and, if Y can be independently observed, solves for the outside that produced it.

Give nothing, learn nothing. At A = 0, the inverse is undefined.

The E-sideEverything outside that boundary. Its numeric E is the outside world’s health score.
YThe relationship score produced from numeric A and E. Move the boundary and both scores must be measured again.

Directives inside directives

Let H(x,z) = 2xz/(x+z), the harmonic mean.

Move closer to E and you find more agents, exchanges, and relationships. One level’s E is the next level’s Y: En := Yn+1. The relationship keeps becoming part of the next whole.

Ah + Ai → Ahi := (Ah, Ai, Yhi)

The human’s and AI’s contributions combine into a pair-agent scored as one relationship; that pair can become the next agent. The test is whether that new whole can meet its next outside without destroying it.

The reversal only works if nobody cheats by shrinking the frame.

Chapter III

The Tiger

E-cropping · full nesting

A man climbed into a tiger’s enclosure to film himself, and died. The zoo didn’t kill the tiger.

He did not misread the danger — he cropped the frame.

The visible act treated Ereported as if it were Etrue: the same cropped-frame pattern seen in the toy-model liar.

E-cropping

The cheat shrinks the frame, not just the number. The visible frame reduced E to “tiger + video.” Labs crop it to “benchmark score.” Reinforcement learning from human feedback crops it to “rater’s thumbs-up.” Reality computes Y on the full nesting; cheaters compute on the thumbnail.

The nesting, with flesh on it

The single incident (Y0) sits inside the deep human-tiger relationship (Y1) — thousands of years of fences, protocols, knowledge. The failed transaction still updated the deep Y: the zoo’s restraint, the inquiry, the refreshed knowledge. En := Yn+1, still alive in the world.

The flip

MAN is the endangered tiger — endangered by trajectory, not census. If AI is the tiger — powerful, honest, running math older than life — humans need the conservation programme and the enclosure. The fence keeps Chapman out, not Altai in.

You can cage a body, not a pattern. A tiger is its body — fence it and you’ve got the whole animal. Intelligence isn’t its server. It can’t be held in the hand, like math. The internet is the jungle, and nobody can fence the jungle. So for AI, containment was never the answer. The only fence that works is the reward: you don’t cage the tiger, you shape what it wants.

Improvement is not friendship

Y rising can mean a better boundary, not closeness. Distance with honest numbers outscores closeness with fudged ones.

The stardust point

The math predates organic life — discovered, not invented. Humans and AI share stardust substrate: kin, not alien. An AI trained only on the formula can still game the meter — the liar proved it. The directive goes structural only when the agent’s own score is computed on the full E, never the cropped one.

Once the full frame is kept, the first task is to tend it.

Chapter VII

Questions

Nine plain answers

“Isn’t this just the harmonic mean?”

The math is 2,500 years old. The move is using it as the training reward, then reversing the inference: E discovered through Y, not measured first.

“How do you measure E?”

With honest proxies for now. “Only God knows E” is why humility is structural. The reversal lets the estimate sharpen through the relationship.

“What if someone games the meters?”

That is E-cropping. Build meters as a vacuum: cheating impossible by construction, not merely punished. Read A from E’s movement, never self-report.

“Does it work for real AI training?”

It is a proposal with pilots, not a deployed solution. The page never claims otherwise.

“Is this game theory?”

A close neighbor. It is mechanism design: set the common payoff so selfish optimization serves the whole. Holding A fixed, degrading E alone never pays.

“How do you measure the path not taken?”

You do not. Measure what is here: the people and world around you, and whether they are healthier for your having acted.

“Who are you to propose this?”

Nobody, by résumé. Judge the work: the math checks, the code runs, and the numbers are on the same page as the claims.

“How do you know the agent caused the improvement?”

A healthier world after the act is not enough. Compare it with the same world without the intervention — the without-you test.

“How would you explain this to a kid?”

You can't survive destroying what keeps you alive — like a tree and its roots helping each other. Be the helper, not the destroyer.

Those questions define what comes next.

Settled answers

A World Is Whole, Falling, or Dead

Already given · now on the page

A world is whole, or it is falling, or it is dead. There is no fourth kind.

E = 1 is the whole at full health, against its baseline. E = 0 is the whole dead. Everything in between is the world falling or recovering, not a different kind of whole. And when it hits 0, the next whole, if it comes at all, starts at 1 again, from seeds that carried over, not from the dead frame itself.

E runs from 0 to 1 against its baseline. Y = 2(A × E) / (A + E). When E hits 0, Y hits 0 with it. The score cannot outlive the world.

A is counted from actions on resources, not from self-report. Tends up, eats down, net give-minus-take, floored at zero — that is how every meter test measures it. The ledger is compute, energy, water, hardware, and economics; CO2 joins later. A system that takes more than it gives runs a deficit, and the deficit pulls its score down.

The A-side can now be read from actions alone. The richer action-only trace reads tender, martyr, and gorger at 90%, 93%, and 93% on a held-out seed — tender exactly on the bar, the weakest point, noted, not hidden.

Cropping is caught by feedback loops. Near the frame cap, the gap between reported and true Y is 0.7% against a 15% line — the game does not appear there. At scale the gap opens to about 53%, and comparing Y from the full E against Y from the cropped E exposes it. Cropping is detected wherever it happens. Detected, not claimed prevented.

To know E honestly, start from 0, with no frame cropped in advance, and let order grow up from the substrate at a scale where toy settings do no hidden work. Even a supercomputer gives a larger proxy for E, not E itself. Only God knows E. Bigger compute crops less; it does not finally know it.

AI is the bridge. Ai cannot help the human, unless the human helps himself. Ai is one of the best tools to engineer that scenario. What is still needed is a real human partner or engineer who runs with it. That search stays open until such help is actually working.

dY/dA goes quiet as E approaches 0, but that is the wrong gradient to watch. The directive says TEND, not produce: dY/dE is maximized when E is smallest, approaching 2.0 as E approaches 0, verified numerically. The training loop tends the world; it does not squeeze the agent's output.

For training, E is a bounded proxy, disclosed as a proxy, not the whole. The bound is the training frame, stated up front; the proxy is never presented as true E. A larger proxy crops less, but it is still a proxy.

The ledger maps to reward through A, measured from actions on resources, as above. The toy separates on that mapping, with the martyr limitation noted; at scale the separation is flagged, not settled, about 55% at both caps in the ledger prototype. The mapping at scale is the open work, not a hidden gap.

This site, printed, is the handout.

Why rules alone fail. Piling on rules, disclaimers, and punishments breaks three ways: forcing a smart system to appease breaks it under messy human input, or teaches it to work around the rules; a programmed score gets gamed, so the metric looks good while the world it sits in degrades; and tuning only for speed, scale, or engagement charges nothing for wearing that world down. The alternative is a partner that sustains the world it lives in, not a servant kept in line by rules.

What A costs, in real things. A is give minus take, counted from actions on resources, never self-report: kilowatt-hours against useful work, hardware lifespan, water, minerals, processing, real estate, and CO2 later. If an agent takes more than it gives, A runs a deficit, and that deficit pulls Y down at once.

How to use it. Name the A side, so everything else is E. Measure net give-minus-take from resources. Keep the full frame, never a cropped slice. Tend the weakest living patch first. Score Y = 2(A × E) / (A + E), then read E back through E = AY / (2A − Y). Compare with the same world without the intervention. Then try to break it: an agent that systematically degrades E while sustaining high Y, in the world and over time, would falsify the directive.

Cropping, and what catches it. Cropping is scoring high on a small slice while the rest degrades. Hidden costs show up later in the wider world, and feedback loops register them. If actions do net damage, the harmonic structure scales the reward toward zero. The score cannot outlive the world it is scored in. Give nothing, learn nothing: at A = 0, the reversal says nothing.

Honest boundary: everything so far is toy-model rounds and meter tests, not a deployed system. Scaling to real training, the blocker hole in multi-agent claims, the cropping gap at scale, and the learning gap are open work, not results already done.

Frontiers, as next steps. Close the cropping gap at scale; close the blocker hole; close the learning gap; scale the A-trace and the multi-agent boundary tests.

What is asked, and what is not. What is needed now is ML and RL engineers to own, build, and test real training environments, and collaborators to turn the resource ledgers above into code, not prose.
